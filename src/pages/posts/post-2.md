It has been a LONG time since I last touched this blog. I made it as a way of documenting the project I was doing, for bringing forward little facts that I have learned, and really whatever else I wanted to write about. But after creating the website and writing my first page, those ambitions were left to rot as I found myself wanting to dedicate my time elsewhere. However, here is another post - out of the blue! I will be detailing what I have been up to this past year - studying, events, awards, realisations, etc. I hope you find it interesting.

### Awards & Changing Strategies
This past year has been quite a wirlwind of discovery and stress. I started the academic year in good stead, trying my new revision methods and soaking up all the information I could get from my lectures. I didn't want to change too much about my revision method, as it handed me the highest grade of the year last year (I am so happy!). However, I found ways to improve it's efficiency. For example, last year I hand-wrote ALL of my flashcards. Each flashcard took anywhere between 3 minutes to 15 minutes to write, each lecture's worth of flashcards taking between 1 and 6 hours to write, and after the academic year I had somewhere over 500 flashcards. Ontop of this, I would then need to revise from these flashcards once they were made. As is probably evident, this took a LOT of my time, and I had to turn down many social events to ensure these were completed. Realising how inefficient this was, I decided that I would change a few things. The biggest was my conversion to Anki.

I had been using hand-written flashcards for at least 6 years at that point (the first time I used them I was hooked), and never entertained the idea of changing. "I learn the most from the writing of the flashcards, so typing would get rid of that benefit". But as I spent so much time trying to write my flashcards quickly, I realised that I no longer learned the most from writing them (in fact, I was barely learning anything from this process anymore). This realisation was the turning point - the benefits of writing were gone, why should I continue to reject the benefits of typing? The scales were tipped in the opposite direction, so during the summer break after year 1 I decided to make the switch to typed flashcards. 

I used Anki to type my flashcards: I had heard it mentioned a few times and after exploring the app and changing a few settings, it seemed perfect for my needs. However, I was so worried for my first exam after the switch: I felt prepared, but what if something about writing flashcards prepared me better for exams? What if I wouldn't be happy with the results? Of course this thinking was silly: I made the switch after evaluating my situation, and knew that I felt prepared enough (I would never let myself wake up to an exam without knowing that my flashcards covered all the content, and that I knew everything written on those flashcards). Nevertheless, I still breathed a sigh of relief when I realised I hadn't messed everything up.

I continued using Anki to create digital flashcards through to my second semester, and reaped the benefits of the reduced time spent writing them. I had more time to spend on my coursework, on my hobbies, and seeing my friends. Last year, I was playing on hard mode. This year, the content levelled up but I felt little difference because of my improved strategy. 

### The Conference
In February, I was very grateful to receive a scholarship ticket to the NorDEV conference: a not-for-profit, volunteer-run tech conference for people in all stages of their career to share their experiences and learn from each other. I learned a lot about old and new technologies, and met some amazing people. 

Being a quiet-natured person, I knew that these two days could be a great way to grow as a person. I made the conscious effort to speak to as many different people as I could. It always makes me smile when I think of one particular interaction: on day 1, I attended a talk by a senior who knew a LOT about C++. Having just completed and REALLY enjoyed my Programming 2 module which taught C++, I knew I wanted to ask him more questions. I spent the rest of day 1 searching for him, building up the courage to pluck him from the crowd and speak to him. It wasn't until the end of day 2 - after endless run-throughs of the possible conversations in my head - that I finally did it! I was so happy and proud of myself.

Although I gained a lot of knowledge from these two days, my biggest takeaway was that growth in my confidence and communication.

### A Reflection, & The Future
After the conference, I spent most of my time doing coursework and exams. I won't bore you with those details, as I already have in the "Awards & Changing Strategies" section of this post. But, now that I have completed another year of University, I can safely say that I have learned a lot. And that the code I wrote for this blog will definitely change because I am cringing at the inefficiency of it (it also definitely could do with a Tailwind revamp, now that I have learned it)! However, it makes me happy to think that I have improved so much that I am embarrased of my past code.

Like last summer, I have quite a few project ideas to be getting on with. For example, I would like to learn Flutter to create my own digital flashcard app, whose database is held on a homemade Oracle server. Maybe I will pick up some of my past projects to revamp and finish them. I will definitely make a start on my third year project (I have already done some research). But most importantly, I will use this time to rest my brain and prepare it for third year!

As before, I hope that this post has been enjoyable. See you next time (whenever that may be)!
